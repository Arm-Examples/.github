# Ten ideas, one weekend, and Edge AI in the real world

It started with a room full of engineers, a collection of Alif Ensemble E8
development boards, and a simple challenge: take an idea beyond the notebook
and make it work at the edge. Over the course of the
[ExecuTorch Hackathon](https://lnkd.in/p/ew3CeDDa), teams turned cameras,
microphones, motion sensors, displays, motors, Cortex-M processors, and
Ethos-U NPUs into working community projects.

**The projects in this collection are based on the
[CMSIS-Executorch template](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/CMSIS-Executorch).**
The template gave every team the same proven foundation for exporting a
PyTorch model, integrating the ExecuTorch runtime, targeting Cortex-M and
Ethos-U, and building an application with CMSIS-Toolbox. From that shared
starting point, the teams took their projects in ten very different directions.

The results range from assistive technology and industrial tools to a tiny
generative companion. Some projects see, some listen, and some do both—but all
of them explore what becomes possible when PyTorch models leave the cloud and
run close to the people, machines, and environments they serve.

Together, the repositories also preserve the engineering journey: the
experiments, trade-offs, measurements, limitations, and lessons from getting
models onto real embedded hardware. They are both a gallery of demos and a set
of starting points for the next engineer with an idea.

## Community contributions

| Project | The idea |
|:--------|:---------|
| [pico-faces conditional diffusion](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/picofaces-conditional-diffusion) | A compact latent-diffusion model that generates 128&times;128 faces on an Ethos-U85, with the Cortex-M55 driving the sampling loop and the board displaying each new result. |
| [CubeTutor](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/CubeTutor) | A camera-based Rubik's Cube tutor that detects a visible face, reads its sticker colours, remembers stable observations, and begins guiding the user toward a white cross. |
| [DCSM-Simplified](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/DCSM-Simplified) | An offline data-centre service assistant that identifies equipment with vision, accepts voice commands, and presents inspection steps and maintenance information without a network connection. |
| [CorteX-ARM](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/CorteX-ARM) | A brain-computer-interface experiment that turns real-time motor-imagery EEG into a predicted hand action, with an IMU used to reject motion artefacts. |
| [Sentinel](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/Sentinel) | A machine monitor that learns a normal sound-and-vibration baseline, stores it on the device, and warns when the observed equipment begins to behave differently. |
| [HandGPT](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/HandGPT) | A best-effort, offline ASL handshape prototype that uses a quantized MobileNetV2 to recognize 24 static letters from the board camera and show predictions on its display. |
| [FingerSpell](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/FingerSpell) | A pocket-sized fingerspelling demonstrator that detects a hand, finds 21 keypoints, recognizes letters, and builds words locally on the board. |
| [Alif Industrial AOI](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/Alif-Industrial-AOI) | An industrial automated-optical-inspection exploration built from the ExecuTorch-on-Ethos-U hackathon starting point. |
| [Alfy](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/Alfy) | A generative on-device companion that can see, hear, react to drawings and touch, and express its changing mood as an animated character generated by the NPU. |
| [WALDO](https://github.com/Arm-Examples/WALDO) | A voice-controlled pan-and-tilt flashlight turret that detects and follows people using two models, two NPUs, and two Cortex-M55 cores on one device. |

Inspired to create your own? [Learn how to build ExecuTorch applications for
